1. House Mode — the brain

The house keeps one overall state, and almost everything follows it. You never normally need to set it — it works itself out from who's home and what's happening.

ModeWhenWhat happens
HomeAt least one of us is in Alarm off, heating comfortable (warmest in the evening)
AwayEveryone out ~20 min, no movement inside Alarm arms fully, heating eases back, camera alerts become urgent
SleepingLate: TVs & lights off, still 20 min Alarm arms the perimeter, heating drops to the night temperature
Coming home from Away: the moment one of us arrives, the house switches to Home and disarms itself — you'll hear "House alarm disarmed." Nothing to do.

On the phone, House Mode is the bar under the rooms on the Home page. Tap it to open, pick a mode; tap outside to close without changing anything.

Storm and Maintenance are automatic — they light up by themselves (Storm during a weather warning, Maintenance while Colm is working on things) and need nothing from you. On the closed bar they show as ⛈ and 🔧, amber when on.

3. Guests staying over

Turn Guest Mode on when anyone is staying: Home → tap the House Mode bar → Guest.

  • The alarm won't fully arm if we both nip out and leave guests in.
  • The heating never drops to the "empty house" setting — guests won't be left cold.
  • Night-time arming still happens, so the house is still secure overnight.

Just remember to turn it off when they leave.

The Guest button is in the House Mode bar. When it’s on it reads Guest on in amber, and 👥 turns amber on the closed bar.

4. Heating & boost

Fully automatic — it eases right back when nobody's home, starts warming when one of us is driving home, and gets up earlier on cold mornings. Today’s plan in Energy → Heating shows the temperatures and times for today, in plain English.

Today’s plan in Energy → Heating — the bar shows the day, the text explains it.

Want it warmer now? Boost.

Tap Boost 2 h on the heating card on Home, or in Energy → Heating, where the Boost temperature slider lets you pick the temperature first. It holds for two hours then returns to the schedule. Cancel ends it early. The hallway dial works too: up = a two-hour boost, down = cancel. No need for the Netatmo app — don't use it.

On your way home? Boost works even when the house is empty — tap Boost in the app before you set off and the house will be warm when you arrive.

While a boost is running, the top line in Energy → Heating says boost … until and the time, and a Cancel button appears on Home.

The ring shows the current temperature (dot = target) and glows orange while the boiler is heating.

Wondering why it just changed?

In Energy → Heating, under Today’s plan, there's a Recent activity list, and every change says the temperature, why it happened, and whether the house did it automatically or someone set it by hand:
  • comfort 19.5° — morning (auto)
  • hot 20° — evening (auto)
  • night 19° — sleep (auto)
  • eco 19° — nobody home (auto)
  • boost 22° — until 15:30 (manual)
Each line shows who: a person’s photo and name means they did it from their phone, and N Node-RED means the house did it itself — Node-RED is the system the house automations run on.
Tap Show all under the list to see the last 24 hours.
So if the heating comes on or eases back and you are not sure what did it, that list will tell you.

The Recent activity list — a photo for a person, N for the house itself.

5. Lights & dimmer switches

Most rooms have a little dimmer switch on the wall for the lights. There are two types around the house — an older square one and a newer rounded one — but they work exactly the same way, with four buttons:

The two types — same four buttons, top to bottom.

ButtonWhat it does
Top — OnTurns the room's lights on. Press it again to cycle through the room's moods (bright, dimmed, night…). Hold for full brightness.
▲ UpBrighter — hold to brighten faster.
▼ DownDimmer — hold to dim faster.
Bottom — OffTurns the room's lights off.

Each dimmer controls the lights in its own room.

Handy to know: the switches are only held to the wall magnetically — you can lift one off its plate and use it like a remote control from the sofa or bed, then just pop it back on the wall when you're done. It works exactly the same off the wall.

6. Alarm

  • Arms and disarms itself with House Mode (see section 1).
  • Front door tag: a backup — disarming normally happens automatically when you arrive; tap your phone on the tag if it hasn't.
  • Shed: tap the back-door tag before going out — it stands the shed alarm down for up to two hours and re-arms after you're done. It also re-arms at 10pm if the shed door is shut.
  • Up late? After 11pm, moving around won’t wake the house — the alarm stays on guard overnight. Walking about inside is fine; it only watches the doors and windows, so you won’t set it off moving between rooms.
  • Window left open? If something's open when it arms for the night, it tells you which one — close it and it arms itself 20 minutes later.
  • If it triggers: every light strobes, everyone's phones alert, and it announces on the speakers. Disarm from the app or the front-door tag; the lights return to normal.

Security → Alarm: Disarmed / Away / Night for the house, Disarm / Away for the shed. Normally you never need them (it follows House Mode), but they're there for manual arming or disarming.

Was a door opened earlier? Tap any door, window or motion sensor in Security → Alarm to see its last 7 days. Tapping never switches anything.

Reading the colours: purple = armed, on guard · grey = disarmed · red = going off right now.

The activity list in Security → Alarm — doors, windows and the alarm, newest first. A person’s photo means they did it; the red Node-RED icon means the house did it itself. Tap Show all for the rest of the day.

8. Cameras & the doorbell

  • Phone alerts with pictures: anyone at the front, doorbell or back garden sends everyone a notification — text, then photo, then a moving clip. Tap it to see that camera live, or tap Clip to watch what it recorded.
  • Doorbell: a person also pops up on the kitchen display and the sitting-room TV, and it says who if the camera recognises them.
  • Someone presses the doorbell: phones ring loudly, even on silent (everyone with Camera / Doorbell on in the Household page). Tap it to open the doorbell: green answers so you can talk, red hangs up.
  • Deliveries: the speakers announce couriers ("DPD has landed…") and the postman.
  • Out the back? Tap the patio door tag and the rear cameras stop alerting while you're gardening — it switches itself off when the patio door is closed again.
  • When Away, camera alerts come through as urgent ⚠️ notifications — and anyone seen at the house rings even if your phone is on silent.
  • While Sleeping, anyone at the cars sets off a light show and a spoken warning — automatic.

Security → CCTV

  • All cameras, live. It opens with every camera live. Tap a camera to make it big.
  • The bar under the cameras is the last 24 hours. Drag it back, up to 10 days, and every camera replays the moment under the blue line. The top says RECORDING while you're looking back; tap Back to live to return.
  • Colours on the bar: grey = movement, amber = alert (a person or car at the house), purple = something spotted, but not an alert.
  • ◀ ▶ jump to the previous or next alert.
  • Under the bar is a list of what happened. Tap one to watch it.

Security → CCTV: cameras, the 24-hour bar, and what happened under it.

The doorbell screen: red hangs up, green answers.

11. Playing music around the house 🎶

We play music through the house speakers with Music Assistant — search for a song, artist or playlist and it plays on whichever speakers you choose.

⚠️ Do this first — it’s not like Spotify.
You have to pick a speaker before anything will play. On a normal music app you just press play; here, if you don’t first choose where the sound should come out, nothing happens. Always choose the device first.
StepWhat to do
1Open Music in the bar (Music Assistant)
2Choose where it plays — tap the speaker picker and pick a speaker, or Home Music Group to play everywhere at once. This is the step people forget — without it, nothing plays
3Search for a song, artist or playlist and press play
4Set the volume — for one speaker, or for the whole group

The Players row along the top is where you choose the speaker — pick one before you press play

Home Music Group plays the two hi-fi speakers and the computer together, in sync — ideal for music right through the house.

Or play music on the hi-fi with the Harmony remote

To play music on the hi-fi in the sitting room or the kitchen, pick up the Harmony remote and choose the “Listen to Music” activity — this switches the hi-fi (the Logitech or Technics) on and controls the volume. You still choose and play the music with Music Assistant on your phone, as above.

12. Make a playlist with AudioMuse 🎧

AudioMuse has listened to all our music and knows how every song sounds. Tell it what you’re in the mood for and it makes a playlist from our own music, then plays it on any speaker.

StepWhat to do
1Open AudioMuse (under More)
2Type what you want under Make a playlist. Or use the Suggestions under the box: tap one to fill the request box, then edit it
3Press Make playlist and look over the songs it picked
4Choose the speaker, then press Save & play

AI pick understands requests like “a journey from one song to another”; Quick search is faster and goes by how the music sounds

Tip: name a few artists. “Irish folk like Planxty, Clannad and Christy Moore” works much better than just “Irish folk”.

Playlists you’ve made are listed under Recent — tap Play to hear one again. They’re also saved to our music library.

13. The night sky 🌙

More → Night sky is a map of the sky over the house, right now: the stars, planets and moon, plus the space station and planes passing over. Use it to find out what you’re looking at outside.

What you’re looking at

#On the map
1The middle is the sky straight above your head
2The edge of the circle is the horizon, all the way round. N, E, S, W tell you which direction each part is
3The ▲ front door marker: stand at the front door looking out, and you’re facing this way
4Names in S P A C E D  C A P I T A L S are star patterns (constellations). The faint lines join up their stars
5Names in small letters are bright stars
6Coloured names are planets
7The moon, drawn as it looks tonight
8The view button — see below

Also on the map, on Now only: a blinking red dot with a flight number is a plane (its short line shows which way it’s flying), and a blue dot marked ISS is the space station.

Find something in the real sky

StepWhat to do
1Go outside and face the way you want to look. Easiest: stand at the front door and look out
2Turn the phone round until the direction you’re facing is at the bottom of the circle. At the front door, turn it until the ▲ is at the bottom
3Now the bottom edge of the circle is the horizon in front of you. Things near the edge are low down; the nearer the middle, the higher up you look
4Tap a star, planet or plane to see its name and how high to look, e.g. “33° up, SW”
How high is 33°? 0° is the horizon and 90° is straight above you. Hold your fist out at arm’s length: one fist is about 10°, so 33° is just over three fists up from the horizon.

Choosing a view

  • Tap the button in the bottom-right corner of the map. It shows the view you’re on, e.g. After dark ▾. Pick Now, After dark, Midnight or Before dawn. (On a computer all four are shown under the map.)
  • Now shows the sky right now. Planes and the space station only show on Now.
  • After dark, Midnight and Before dawn show what the sky will look like later tonight. After a few minutes it goes back by itself.
  • In daylight it opens on After dark, because you can’t see the stars during the day.

Underneath

CardWhat it tells you
MoonHow full it is, and when it rises and sets
Darkness tonightWhen it gets truly dark, and the cloud forecast: Clear tonight, Some gaps or Cloudy
PlanetsWhich planets are up, and the best time to see the others
Space stationWhen it next passes over the house
Shooting starsThe next meteor shower
Going out to look? Check Darkness tonight first — if it says Cloudy, you won’t see much.

15. The music quiz 🎵

A game for everyone: the sitting room TV plays the first few seconds of a song from our own collection, and you race to name it on your phone.

Open this on your phone (works only on our wifi):

🎧 Open the Intro Quiz

StepWhat to do
1Everyone taps the button above and types their name
2While waiting, pick 3 artists you know from the wall — a song from each player’s picks sneaks into the game (nobody knows which rounds!)
3The game master taps Start — the scoreboard and the music appear on the sitting room TV (it turns on by itself)
4Listen… then tap the right song on your phone — faster answers score more!
5Stuck? Anyone can tap “play a bit more” to hear extra seconds — one go at a time: the button unlocks again once the longer clip finishes
6After each round: the album cover and a proper chunk of the song — it plays in full before the next round can start, so enjoy it
7Half time (after round 5): everyone’s phone shows a music fact to read out to the table, then three quick true-or-false questions — worth +50 points each on the scoreboard!

One phone is the game master — it runs the rounds, and a 🎤 banner on every phone shows who it is. The master changes every game: the final scores screen announces who runs the next one.

Ten rounds a game. Every game counts towards the all-time leaderboard, and the TV now shows it — while everyone’s joining, and again beside the final scores. The bragging rights are permanent (and public). If a clip is rubbish (just clapping, say), tap “🚫 bad clip” and it will never appear again.

Good to know: the start screen also has a “no scoreboard” choice — then the music plays from the sitting room speaker instead of the TV. And if a game gets abandoned mid-way (dinner’s ready), tap “abandon game” at the top of your phone screen — no scores are kept for unfinished games.
